Insurance glossary

Insurance has its own language. Here are the terms you’ll meet most often in quotes, policy wordings and claims, in plain English, including the US equivalents.

Photo: Cytonn Photography on Unsplash

Policy basics

Policyholder
The person or business that owns the policy.
Premium
What you pay for the policy, usually yearly or monthly.
Sum insured
The maximum the insurer will pay for a section of cover, such as buildings or contents.
Limit of indemnity
The maximum a liability policy will pay for a claim or in a period.
Policy wording
The full contract: definitions, cover, exclusions and conditions.
Schedule
The page personal to you: who and what is insured, sums insured, excesses and dates.
IPID
Insurance Product Information Document: a short standardised summary of a general insurance product (UK and EU).
Endorsement
A change or addition to the standard policy wording, sometimes called a rider in the US.
Peril
An event that causes loss, such as fire, storm or theft. “Named perils” policies list them; “open perils” policies cover everything not excluded.

Money terms

Excess (deductible)
The part of a claim you pay. UK policies have compulsory and voluntary excesses; US policies call it a deductible.
Average clause
A condition that reduces a claim in proportion if you were under-insured.
New for old
Replacing items with new equivalents rather than paying their second-hand value.
Indemnity
Putting you back in the same financial position as before the loss, no better and no worse.
Insurance Premium Tax (IPT)
UK tax on most general insurance premiums: 12% standard rate, 20% higher rate for some products such as travel insurance.
Premium finance
Credit used to spread premium payments monthly, which can add interest.
No claims discount
A discount for claim-free years, also called no claims bonus or bonus-malus.

Claims terms

Loss adjuster
An expert appointed by the insurer to investigate and value a larger claim.
Loss assessor
An expert you can appoint (and pay) to prepare and negotiate your claim.
Subrogation
The insurer’s right, after paying you, to recover the cost from whoever caused the loss.
Contribution
Sharing a claim between two insurers when more than one policy covers the same loss.
Settlement
The final agreed payment or repair for a claim.
Final response
An insurer’s last word on a complaint, after which UK customers can go to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

Business insurance terms

Claims-made
A policy that responds to claims made while it’s in force (typical for PI and D&O).
Occurrence basis
A policy that responds to incidents that happened while it was in force, whenever the claim is made (typical for public liability).
Retroactive date
Work done before this date isn’t covered by a claims-made policy.
Run-off cover
Protection for claims about past work after a business stops trading.
Indemnity period
In business interruption cover, the maximum time the policy pays lost income while you recover.
Gross profit (insurance)
Turnover minus specified variable costs; the usual basis for business interruption cover.
Fair presentation
A business’s legal duty under the UK Insurance Act 2015 to disclose material facts clearly.
Additional insured
Another party (such as a client) given protection under your liability policy.

US terms

HO-3 / HO-5
Standard US homeowners policy forms; HO-5 is broader for belongings.
HO-4 / HO-6
Renters and condo owner policy forms.
BOP
Business owner’s policy: property, general liability and business income bundled.
CGL
Commercial general liability.
PIP
Personal injury protection, required in no-fault auto insurance states.
Experience mod
A multiplier in workers’ comp pricing based on your claims history.
NFIP
National Flood Insurance Program, run by FEMA.
