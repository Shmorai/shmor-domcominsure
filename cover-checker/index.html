Legal or lender requirement

Buildings insurance

Your mortgage lender will almost certainly require it as a condition of the loan.

Buildings insurance

Without a lender insisting, it’s your call, but rebuilding a home is beyond most people’s savings.

Contents insurance

Covers your belongings against theft, fire and water damage. Often combined with buildings cover.

Read the guide → Strongly consider

Renters / tenants insurance

Your landlord’s policy doesn’t cover your belongings or your liability. In the US, leases often require it.

Read the guide → Legal or lender requirement

Tenants’ insurance (rental risks)

Some countries require it: in France, tenants must insure against rental risks. Check your lease and national rules.

Read the guide → Strongly consider

Landlord insurance

Standard home insurance usually stops covering a property once it’s let. Covers the building, liability and loss of rent.

Read the guide → Strongly consider

Flood insurance (NFIP or private)

Standard US homeowners policies exclude flood. Required for federally backed mortgages in high-risk zones. Mind the 30-day wait.

Check flood cover and Flood Re

UK home policies usually include flood; if you struggle to find cover, Flood Re may help eligible homes built before 2009.

Read the guide → Worth a look

Check natural catastrophe cover

Flood cover and national catastrophe schemes vary by country. Check your home policy wording.

Car insurance

Third-party cover is a legal minimum to drive in the UK, the EU and almost every US state.

Read the guide → Strongly consider

Life insurance

Pays a lump sum or income so your family can cover the mortgage and living costs if you die.

Read the guide → Strongly consider

Travel insurance

Medical costs abroad can be huge. Consider an annual policy if you travel more than once or twice a year.

Read the guide → Legal or lender requirement

Employers’ liability insurance

A legal requirement for most UK employers: at least £5m cover, with fines of up to £2,500 a day without it.

Read the guide → Legal or lender requirement

Workers’ compensation

Required in almost every state once you have employees (Texas is the main exception).

Read the guide → Legal or lender requirement

Employer accident & liability cover

Many EU countries require employer contributions to statutory accident insurance. Check national rules for your staff.

Read the guide → Strongly consider

Public / general liability

Covers injury or damage to customers and the public. Clients, venues and landlords often require it.

Read the guide → Strongly consider

Professional indemnity (E&O)

Covers claims that your advice or work caused a client financial loss. Compulsory for some professions.

Read the guide → Strongly consider

Tradesperson insurance package

Combines liability, tools and contract works cover. Main contractors often ask for certificates.

Read the guide → Strongly consider

Commercial property & stock

Protects premises, equipment and stock. Leases and lenders usually require it.

Read the guide → Worth a look

Business interruption

Replaces lost profit while you recover from insured damage. Pick a long enough indemnity period.

Read the guide → Worth a look

Business owner’s policy (BOP)

Bundles property, general liability and business income for many small US firms, often for less.

Read the guide → Worth a look

Cyber insurance

Pays for incident response, downtime and claims after a data breach or ransomware attack.

Read the guide → Legal or lender requirement

Van insurance (business use)

Vehicles need at least third-party cover, with the right class of business use.

Read the guide → Legal or lender requirement

Fleet insurance

Motor cover is compulsory; with three or more vehicles a fleet policy is often simpler and cheaper.

Read the guide → Worth a look

Directors & officers (D&O)

Protects directors’ personal assets from claims about how they run the company.

Read the guide → Strongly consider

Home business cover

Tell your home insurer about business use; stock, equipment and visitors may need extra cover.

How the checker works

The checker matches your answers against common legal requirements and the covers lenders, landlords and clients typically expect. It’s deliberately cautious and general: it can’t see your contracts, your lender’s conditions or your exact location, and it isn’t personal advice. Use it as a shortlist, then read each guide and check the policy wording.

What the labels mean

  • Legal or lender requirement: required by law in your region (such as motor or employers’ cover), or almost always required by a lender.
  • Strongly consider: commonly needed for your situation or demanded by clients, leases or landlords.
  • Worth a look: depends on your risks and budget.
