How to make a home insurance claim

A claim is when your policy proves its worth. Being organised from the first hour makes it faster and fairer. Here’s a practical walk-through.

By the DomCom Insure editorial team · 11 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: Romain Dancre on Unsplash

On this page

  1. The first hour
  2. Evidence
  3. Loss adjusters and assessors
  4. Settlement and disputes

The first hour

  • Make sure everyone is safe; call emergency services if needed.
  • Prevent further damage if it’s safe: stop the water, cover openings.
  • For theft or vandalism, report to the police and get a crime reference.
  • Call your insurer’s claims line; ask about emergency repairs and alternative accommodation.

Evidence

Photos and video of damage, receipts and bank statements, manuals, valuations and pre-loss photos of rooms. Don’t throw away damaged items until the insurer agrees. Keep a log of calls, names and dates.

Loss adjusters and assessors

For larger claims, the insurer may appoint a loss adjuster to investigate and value the loss. You can appoint a loss assessor to act for you; they charge a fee, often a percentage of the settlement, so think about whether you need one.

Settlement and disputes

Check the offer against your policy: is it new-for-old, has an excess been deducted, has average been applied? Ask for the calculation in writing. If you disagree, use the complaints process, and in the UK the Financial Ombudsman Service. See claims and complaints.

Last updated: 11 October 2026

Frequently asked questions

How long do I have to report a claim?
Policies usually require you to report “as soon as reasonably possible”. Late reporting can cause problems, especially if damage got worse.
Can I start repairs before the insurer agrees?
Emergency repairs to prevent further damage are usually fine; keep receipts. Wait for agreement before permanent repairs.
