Under-insurance and the average clause

Saving money by lowering your sum insured can backfire badly. Many home and business policies include an “average” condition that reduces claims in proportion when you’re under-insured.

By the DomCom Insure editorial team · 20 September 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: Jakub Żerdzicki on Unsplash

How average works

If the sum insured is lower than the true value at the time of the loss, the insurer pays only the same proportion of the claim. A simple example with round numbers:

Amount
True replacement value of contents60,000
Sum insured40,000 (two-thirds)
Claim after a kitchen fire9,000
Payout under average6,000 (two-thirds), before the excess

This is an illustration, not a prediction: wordings differ, and some policies don’t apply average at all or only when under-insurance exceeds a margin.

Why under-insurance happens

  • Buildings insured for market value instead of rebuild cost, or not updated as costs rise.
  • Contents totals guessed rather than counted.
  • Business stock that grows seasonally.
  • Business interruption based on turnover or last year’s profit, with too short an indemnity period.

How to avoid it

  1. Recalculate sums at each renewal, and after renovations or big purchases.
  2. Use the BCIS calculator or a surveyor for UK rebuild costs.
  3. Do a room-by-room contents inventory.
  4. Ask whether index-linking or “day one” reinstatement is available on business property.

Last updated: 20 September 2026

Frequently asked questions

Do all policies have an average clause?
No. Some “unlimited” or high-limit contents policies don’t, and wordings vary. Check the conditions section.
Does average apply to liability cover?
Average typically applies to property sums insured. Liability limits work differently: claims above the limit aren’t covered.
