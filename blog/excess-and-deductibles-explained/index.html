Excesses and deductibles explained

The excess (deductible in the US) is the part of a claim you pay yourself. Choosing it well can lower your premium; choosing it badly can make a policy almost useless.

By the DomCom Insure editorial team · 4 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: Jakub Żerdzicki on Unsplash

On this page

  1. Compulsory and voluntary
  2. How deductibles work in the US
  3. Choosing an excess
  4. Business policies

Compulsory and voluntary

UK policies usually have a compulsory excess set by the insurer and an optional voluntary excess you choose. They add together. Certain perils often carry their own higher excess: subsidence on buildings cover, escape of water on some home policies, or young drivers on car insurance.

How deductibles work in the US

US policies use a deductible per claim (home and auto). Some perils use percentage deductibles based on the dwelling limit, such as hurricane or windstorm deductibles in coastal states. See hurricane deductibles.

Choosing an excess

  • Pick a total you could pay tomorrow without hardship.
  • Compare quotes at the same total excess.
  • Remember that small claims below or near the excess aren’t worth making and can affect future premiums.

Business policies

Business policies may have excesses per claim, per section, or aggregate deductibles for the year. Liability policies sometimes apply an excess only to property damage claims. Check the schedule for each section.

Frequently asked questions

Does a higher excess always reduce the premium?
Usually, but not always by much. Check the saving is worth the extra risk.
Do I pay the excess if the accident wasn’t my fault?
Often you pay it first and may recover it if your insurer recovers costs from the other side.
