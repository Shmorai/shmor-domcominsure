What home insurance doesn’t cover: gaps to check before you claim

Most home insurance disputes are not about whether you have a policy. They are about what the policy actually covers. Knowing the common gaps before something goes wrong makes claims smoother.

By the DomCom Insure editorial team · 18 November 2026 · 3 min read

Your policy wording decides what is covered

The Financial Ombudsman Service, which settles insurance disputes in the UK, says the policy wording makes clear what you are covered for. When an insurer refuses or reduces a payment by relying on an exclusion, the Ombudsman looks at whether it has applied that exclusion fairly. So the first step is always to read the exclusions section and the definitions, not just the summary.

Home insurance commonly protects against things such as accidental damage, weather, flooding, ground movement and underground pipes, but each policy sets its own limits. Our guide to home insurance explains the main building blocks.

Buildings or contents? The line is not always obvious

Disputes often start with a simple question: is the damaged item part of the building or part of your contents? According to the Financial Ombudsman:

  • Buildings cover the structure and permanent fixtures and fittings, such as baths and fitted kitchens. Garages and sheds are usually included, but check your policy.
  • Contents cover possessions you would take with you if you moved house.
  • Fitted carpets are treated as contents because they can be lifted and relaid.
  • Glued laminate flooring is usually treated as a fixture, while click-together laminate may be treated as contents.

You can buy buildings-only or contents-only cover, even from different insurers, so make sure nothing falls between the two. See buildings insurance and contents insurance.

Items that usually need a separate policy

The Ombudsman notes that motorised vehicles such as e-scooters usually need a specialist policy rather than home insurance. As a rule, anything with its own dedicated insurance market, such as vehicles, is worth checking separately, and high-value items may need to be listed individually. Always confirm in your wording rather than assuming.

Gradual damage, repairs and settlements

Gradual damage is listed by the Ombudsman as its own complaint area, which is a reminder that damage building up over time is treated differently from a sudden event. Other recurring complaint themes include repairs that do not fix the problem, a repair being offered when you want a replacement, and cash settlements too low to cover the work. Photograph damage, keep receipts and read how your policy settles claims before you accept an offer. Our claims guide walks through the process.

Underinsurance, misstatements and empty homes

The same list of complaint areas includes underinsurance, where the insured value is judged too low to cover the claim, and policies cancelled because the insurer believes replacement or rebuild costs were misstated. Read how the average clause works and why accurate answers matter. Storm, flood, subsidence and unoccupied property claims also appear often, so check any conditions on leaving a home empty.

If a claim is refused

Complain to your insurer first. The Financial Ombudsman says insurers must generally give a final response within 8 weeks for most complaints. If you are still unhappy, you may be able to refer the complaint to the Ombudsman, which aims to put you back in the position you would have been in if you were treated unfairly. This applies to UK policies; in other countries, contact the relevant state or national regulator.

Frequently asked questions

Does home insurance cover everything that happens in my house?
No. Every policy has exclusions, limits and conditions. The wording tells you what is covered, so read the exclusions and definitions before you need to claim.
Are fitted carpets buildings or contents?
The Financial Ombudsman says fitted carpets are treated as contents because they can be lifted and relaid. Check how your own policy defines them.
How long does an insurer have to respond to a complaint?
For most UK complaints, the Financial Ombudsman says the insurer must give a final response within 8 weeks. After that you may be able to go to the Ombudsman.
