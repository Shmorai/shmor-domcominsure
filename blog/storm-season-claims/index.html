Storm damage and your home insurance

The 2026/27 storm naming season began in September. If a storm damages your home, knowing what your insurer considers a “storm” and what it usually excludes can make the claim much smoother.

By the DomCom Insure editorial team · 10 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: Max LaRochelle on Unsplash

What counts as a storm?

Many insurers define a storm by wind speed, heavy rainfall, snow or hail, and may check weather records for your area on the date. Named storms are a useful signal but naming isn’t a requirement for a storm claim. The Met Office, Met Éireann and KNMI name storms expected to have medium or high impacts; this season’s list begins Austen, Boelo, Chloe.

Usually covered vs usually excluded

Usually coveredUsually excluded
Roof tiles blown off by high windsDamage from wear and tear or poor maintenance
Water getting in through storm damageFences, gates and hedges (common exclusion)
Falling trees damaging the houseGradual water ingress through old pointing
Contents damaged as a resultRemoving a fallen tree that didn’t damage anything (often)

Claiming

  1. Make safe and prevent further damage (tarpaulins, buckets) if it’s safe.
  2. Photograph everything, including the wider scene.
  3. Report promptly and keep receipts for emergency work.
  4. Expect a surveyor or loss adjuster for larger claims.

Frequently asked questions

Will my claim be refused if my roof was old?
If the damage was caused mainly by wear and tear rather than the storm, cover may not apply. Regular maintenance helps.
Is flood from a storm covered?
In the UK, flood is usually covered by standard home insurance. In the US, flood needs a separate policy.
