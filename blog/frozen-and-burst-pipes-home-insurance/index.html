Frozen and burst pipes: what home insurance usually covers and what to do first

A single burst pipe can flood a home in minutes. When temperatures drop, escape of water is one of the costliest and most common causes of home insurance claims. Here is what to do if a pipe freezes or bursts, how insurers usually treat the claim and how to lower the risk.

Does home insurance cover burst pipes?

In the UK, a burst pipe is normally treated as escape of water, which is a standard insured event under most buildings and contents policies. Buildings cover generally deals with damage to the structure, fixtures and the plumbing; contents cover with your belongings. See our guides to buildings insurance and contents insurance.

Cover is not unconditional. Common conditions include a higher excess for escape of water, exclusions for gradual leaks and wear and tear, and rules about empty or unoccupied homes (often 30 days or more) and keeping the property reasonably heated. Policies differ, so check your own wording. Our unoccupied property guide and what home insurance doesn't cover explain the common gaps. In the US, burst pipes are usually covered by homeowners policies when the damage is sudden and accidental, but not for neglect or maintenance failures; confirm with your insurer.

Why it matters: the cost of a burst pipe

The Association of British Insurers (ABI) said insurers settled roughly 12,000 weather-related burst pipe claims in 2023, with the average claim above £17,000, according to a January 2025 report. Escape of water claims of all kinds across homes and businesses totalled £987 million in 2022. These are past figures and change each year, but they show why insurers keep reminding customers to take simple precautions.

If a pipe freezes: thaw it safely

  • Turn off the water at the main stopcock (often under the kitchen sink) and open the nearest tap so the water can flow once the ice melts.
  • Use a hot water bottle or a thick cloth soaked in hot water over the frozen section, working from the tap end back along the pipe. A hairdryer on a low setting is another option used by local-authority guidance.
  • Never use a naked flame or blowtorch. It can permanently damage pipes and start a fire.
  • Check for cracks and leaks as it thaws. A pipe that has split can start leaking only when the ice melts.

If a pipe bursts: the first 10 minutes

  1. Turn off the main stopcock. If you can't stop the flow, open all the cold taps to drain the system.
  2. If water has reached electrics, switch the power off at the consumer unit only if it's dry and safe to reach, and keep away from wet sockets and the fuse box if it is wet. Call an electrician.
  3. Move valuables and furniture out of the way, and use towels and buckets to limit the damage.
  4. Photograph and film the damage before you clear up. Keep damaged items, receipts and the plumber's invoice.
  5. Call your insurer's claims line. Many have a 24-hour number; some policies include emergency help or a home emergency add-on, see home emergency cover explained.

How to lower the risk of frozen pipes

Advice from the ABI and Northern Ireland's nidirect includes:

  • Know where your stopcock is and that it turns. Keep a plumber's number handy.
  • Lag pipes and tanks in lofts, garages and other cold spots, and fix dripping taps.
  • Keep the heating on low, even when you're away. A frost setting on the thermostat is a simple precaution.
  • In extreme cold, leave the loft hatch open so warm air reaches un-lagged tanks and pipes, and never put heaters in the roof space.
  • Going away for Christmas? Ask someone to check the house, or ask your insurer what it requires if the home will be empty. Our winter home insurance checklist has more.

Frequently asked questions

Is a frozen pipe covered by home insurance?
Damage caused when a frozen pipe bursts is usually covered as escape of water, subject to the policy's terms, excess and conditions such as keeping the home heated. The pipe repair itself may be treated differently, so read your wording.
What if I'm away and the pipes freeze?
Many policies restrict cover if a home is unoccupied for 30 days or more, or require heating or draining the water. Tell your insurer before you go if the home will be empty.
Will a burst pipe claim raise my premium?
It can, depending on the insurer and your claims history. Escape of water claims may also carry a higher excess. Ask your insurer before you claim for small amounts.
Should I call a plumber or my insurer first?
Stop the water first, then call your insurer. Many have approved repairers or emergency lines, and they may want to see the damage before clearing up. Keep receipts.
