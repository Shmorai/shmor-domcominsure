Hurricane deductibles explained

In hurricane-prone US states, wind damage from a hurricane can carry a separate, percentage-based deductible that’s much larger than your usual one. Know yours before storm season.

By the DomCom Insure editorial team · 7 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: NASA on Unsplash

On this page

  1. What they are
  2. A worked example
  3. Triggers
  4. Flood is separate

What they are

According to the Insurance Information Institute, 19 states and the District of Columbia have hurricane deductibles. Instead of a fixed dollar amount, they’re usually a percentage of the home’s insured value (the dwelling limit), typically from 1% to 5%, and can be higher in some high-risk coastal areas.

A worked example

With a dwelling limit of $300,000 and a 2% hurricane deductible, you’d pay the first $6,000 of a hurricane wind claim. A standard $1,000 deductible might still apply to other claims such as fire or theft.

Triggers

Triggers vary by state and insurer: they may depend on the National Weather Service naming a storm, issuing a hurricane watch or warning, or a storm reaching a certain intensity, and they often apply for a window before and after landfall. Read the endorsement in your policy.

Flood is separate

Storm surge and rising water are flood, which standard homeowners policies exclude. You need NFIP or private flood cover. See flood insurance and the NFIP deadline.

Frequently asked questions

Can I choose a lower hurricane deductible?
Sometimes, for a higher premium. Options depend on your state and insurer.
Does a tropical storm trigger the hurricane deductible?
It depends on the trigger definition in your policy and state rules.
