Working from home and your insurance

Hybrid working is normal now, but home insurance policies still draw a line between “clerical work” and running a business. Here’s how to tell which side you’re on.

By the DomCom Insure editorial team · 30 September 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: Mikey Harris on Unsplash

Employees working from home

Many home insurers accept occasional or regular clerical work by employees without any change, but it’s worth checking. Equipment provided by your employer is usually their responsibility, not your home policy’s. If colleagues or clients visit, or you keep company stock at home, tell your insurer.

Running your own business from home

If you’re self-employed or run a company from home, tell your insurer. Depending on what you do, you might need extra cover for business equipment, stock, money, visitors and liability. See home business insurance for a cover-by-cover breakdown.

Quick self-check

  • Do clients or customers visit? → public liability.
  • Do you keep stock or tools? → stock or business contents cover.
  • Do you employ anyone at home? → employers’ liability / workers’ comp.
  • Do you hold customer data? → cyber.
  • Do you give advice? → professional indemnity.

Last updated: 30 September 2026

Frequently asked questions

Will working from home increase my home insurance?
Occasional clerical work often makes no difference. Running a business with visitors or stock may change the price or need a separate policy.
Is my personal laptop covered when I use it for work?
Often yes at home, but limits for business equipment can apply. Check the wording.
