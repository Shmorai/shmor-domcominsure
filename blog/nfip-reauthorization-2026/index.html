NFIP reauthorization: what the next deadline means for flood cover

The US National Flood Insurance Program runs on short-term extensions from Congress. As of mid-October 2026 its authorization runs to 11 December 2026. Here’s what a lapse would and wouldn’t affect.

By the DomCom Insure editorial team · 14 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: Wes Warren on Unsplash

Key points

Current deadline
11 December 2026 (as of 15 October 2026).
Existing policies
Continue to the end of their term if a lapse happens.
At risk in a lapse
New policies and renewals; some home sales and refinances in flood zones.
Waiting period
New NFIP policies usually take 30 days to start.

Where things stand

The NFIP needs Congress to renew its legal authority to issue new policies. Since 2017 it has been extended many times, mostly for short periods. A Congressional Research Service update in September 2026 reported authorization to 11 December 2026. Deadlines can change quickly, so check FEMA or Congress for the latest.

The program briefly lapsed in autumn 2025 during a federal government shutdown, before being restored retroactively.

What a lapse means

  • Existing policies stay in force until their expiry date, and claims are still paid.
  • New policies and renewals can’t be issued while authority has lapsed, so policies expiring during a lapse may not renew on time.
  • Property deals in high-risk flood zones that need flood insurance for a federally backed mortgage can be delayed.
  • The NFIP’s ability to borrow from the Treasury is reduced.

What homeowners can do

  1. Check your NFIP renewal date and renew early if it falls near a deadline.
  2. Remember the 30-day waiting period for new policies.
  3. Ask about private flood insurance, which isn’t affected by NFIP authorization and is available in many states.
  4. Keep flood cover in place even if your mortgage doesn’t require it: standard homeowners policies exclude flood.

Frequently asked questions

Will my claim be paid if the NFIP lapses?
Claims under existing policies continue to be paid, although a prolonged lapse alongside limited borrowing could affect timing.
Is private flood insurance a good alternative?
It can be, depending on your state and property. Compare cover, limits and waiting periods with NFIP options.
