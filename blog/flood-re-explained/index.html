Flood Re explained

If you live somewhere at high flood risk in the UK, you may have benefited from Flood Re without knowing it. Here’s how the scheme works and whether your home qualifies.

By the DomCom Insure editorial team · 18 September 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: Chris Gallagher on Unsplash

Key points

What it is
A reinsurance scheme run jointly by government and insurers.
How you use it
Through your normal home insurer; you can’t buy from Flood Re directly.
Build date
Homes built before 1 January 2009 can be eligible.
Ends
Designed to close in 2039.

How it works

Insurers can pass (“cede”) the flood part of an eligible home policy to Flood Re for a set price based on the home’s council tax band. That lets them offer cover at prices they might not otherwise. All UK home insurers pay a levy that funds the scheme.

Who is eligible

Broadly, a home must be residential, in council tax bands A–H (or equivalent), insured by the occupants or left empty, and built before 1 January 2009. Some types of property and policy, including certain landlord, leasehold block and commercial arrangements, aren’t eligible. Flood Re’s own website has an eligibility checker and a list of insurers using the scheme.

Why 2009 and 2039?

Excluding newer homes is meant to discourage building in flood-prone areas without proper protection. Flood Re is designed as a transition: by 2039 the aim is for flood insurance to be affordable through a combination of better flood defences, resilient homes and risk-reflective pricing. That’s why Flood Re also promotes property flood resilience, such as through “Build Back Better” payments some insurers offer after a flood.

Last updated: 18 September 2026

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to apply to Flood Re?
No. Buy home insurance as usual; if your insurer uses Flood Re and your home is eligible, it can use the scheme behind the scenes.
Does Flood Re cover businesses?
No. It’s for eligible households only.
