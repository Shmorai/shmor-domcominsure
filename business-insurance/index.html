Business insurance for small firms

Commercial insurance isn’t one policy: it’s a set of covers you combine to match what your business does. Some are legal requirements, some are demanded by clients or landlords, and some simply stop one bad day closing the business.

How to build the right cover

  1. List what you do: where you work, who visits, what you sell or advise on, what you own, and how you use vehicles.
  2. Tick off legal requirements: staff and vehicles first.
  3. Check contracts: clients, venues, landlords and platforms often specify minimum liability limits (for example £5m or $1m per occurrence).
  4. Insure your assets: premises, equipment, stock and the income they generate.
  5. Think about your biggest “what if”: a data breach, a design error, a fire, a key supplier failing.

Packages vs separate policies

Many insurers sell packages for particular trades (shops, offices, salons, restaurants, tradespeople) that bundle liability, contents, stock and business interruption. In the US the equivalent for small firms is the business owner’s policy (BOP). Packages are convenient, but check each section’s limits and exclusions; separate specialist policies can be better for professional indemnity and cyber.

Disclosure rules for business insurance

In the UK, the Insurance Act 2015 requires businesses to make a fair presentation of the risk: disclose everything you know or ought to know that would influence an insurer, in a clear way. If you don’t, the insurer’s remedy depends on what it would have done had it known, which can mean a reduced claim. Keep records of what you told the insurer and update them when the business changes. See disclosure and misrepresentation.

Brokers or direct?

Simple risks (a sole-trader consultant, a small shop) are easy to buy online. A broker adds value when your business is unusual, larger, or needs several covers coordinated, and can negotiate wordings and help with claims. In the UK, check brokers and insurers on the FCA register; in the US, check the agent’s license with your state insurance department.

Frequently asked questions

What insurance does a small business legally need?
In the UK, employers’ liability if you have employees, and motor insurance for vehicles. In the US, workers’ compensation in almost every state once you have employees, plus commercial auto for business vehicles. Other covers are usually required by contracts rather than law.
Do sole traders need business insurance?
Not legally for most activities without staff, but public liability and professional indemnity are often required by clients. Your home and car policies may not cover business use.
Is business insurance tax-deductible?
Business insurance premiums are generally an allowable business expense in the UK and deductible as an ordinary business expense in the US. Check with your accountant.
